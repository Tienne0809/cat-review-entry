# Notes — Cat Reviews Its Human

The line (141 characters):

> One star. The human feeds me on his schedule, not mine, and calls my 3 a.m. concerts 'noise.' Service: unacceptable. Ambience: a closed door.

Why it is built this way:

- **Real review shape first.** It opens with the star rating spelled out ("One star."), then lists specific service failures, then closes with the category-style verdicts a real one-star review uses ("Service: unacceptable. Ambience: a closed door."). The joke lands because the format never winks.
- **The grievances are a cat's, not a person's.** Food served on the human's schedule instead of the cat's is the classic feline complaint; the 3 a.m. "concerts" line flips the usual noise complaint — the cat is genuinely proud of the singing the human keeps criticizing; and the closed door is the unforgivable ambience failure in any house a cat owns.
- **Deadpan over punchline.** No exclamation, no "as a cat" framing, no explanation. Each clause is a separate, calmly itemized disappointment, which is what makes a one-star review funny to read.
- **Constraints.** One line, 141 characters (limit 200), explicitly one star — not two, not five.
